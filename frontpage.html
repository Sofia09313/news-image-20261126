Trade — Washington

U.S. Unveils New Tariffs on 14 Trading Partners

Washington aims its latest levies at what it calls structural excess industrial capacity, with the highest rates reserved for China.

Washington — The United States on Wednesday proposed a fresh round of tariffs on 14 major trading partners, a move the administration says is needed to counter subsidised production and factories churning out more goods than world markets can absorb.

The highest rates would fall on China, which Washington blames most for flooding global markets with cheap exports. The European Union and Brazil are also among those in line for higher duties, drawing an angry response from European officials.

The announcement signals that trade tensions will run into the new year, with exporters bracing for retaliation and importers warning that the levies will feed through to prices on shop shelves.
